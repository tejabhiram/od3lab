Ocular Drug Delivery and Discovery Lab, UIC

Getting the right drug to the right place in the eye, and keeping it there.

We engineer sustained-release and minimally invasive delivery systems for the eye, and we use machine learning on the host response to identify the pathogens behind ocular infection. Our goal is fewer drops, fewer injections and better outcomes for patients.

What we do

Four connected research programs

Each program pairs a delivery problem with a disease the eye clinic sees every day, from herpetic keratitis to post-surgical inflammation.

NEI R01

Sustained topical delivery

DECON, our drug-encapsulated carbon platform, holds corticosteroids and antivirals on the ocular surface and releases them over days, so a single dose can replace many eye drops.

Read more
Microneedles

Dissolving corneal microneedles

O-GPT is a dissolvable microneedle film that places drug directly into the corneal stroma, improving compliance and cutting the burden of frequent dosing.

Read more
UIC CCTS pilot

Polymeric depots for the back of the eye

PLGA microspheres, thin films and electrospun fibers deliver antivirals, antibiotics and steroids for weeks, targeting herpetic uveitis and post-surgical complications.

Read more
Machine learning

Host-directed pathogen classification

We train machine learning models on the host's transcriptional response to infection to identify whether a viral, fungal or bacterial pathogen is to blame, even when too little pathogen is present to detect directly.
